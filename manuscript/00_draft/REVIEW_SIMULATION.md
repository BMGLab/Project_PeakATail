# Simulated referee report — Genome Biology, Method track

**Manuscript:** "How much of a single-cell poly(A)-site call set can be trusted? Precision-first calling, calibrated switch testing and pre-registered evaluation in PeakATail"

**Reviewer stance:** I work on single-cell 3′-end methods and APA statistics. I came to this manuscript expecting the usual pattern — a new caller, a curated-atlas benchmark it wins, a differential test whose null behaviour is never shown — and I did not find that pattern. This is one of the more honest method papers I have refereed. It is also not yet ready: the paper's own best argument (that atlas benchmarking is gameable) is left half-applied to its own headline claim, several load-bearing numbers rest on infrastructure the paper itself declares unfinished (un-deduplicated long-read truth, a PAS-to-gene assignment under revision, two supplements "in preparation"), and the cohort null has only ten permutations under a zero-replication headline.

---

## 1. Three things I would praise

**P1 — The calibration section is the best thing in the paper (Fig 4; Results "The switch test is calibrated...").** The authors ran their own shipped differential test under label permutation, found it anti-conservative (13.0–24.7% null p < 0.05), shipped the one configuration that passes, and — crucially — decomposed the mechanisms: marker pre-selection as a label double-dip (with the 17.4% reconstruction experiment), read-count Fisher as UMI pseudoreplication, and the NB dispersion floor carrying 67% of null q-hits from 8.5% of tests. I know of no single-cell APA tool paper that has done this to its own defaults. The pre-stated operational definition of "calibrated", flagged as theirs and not a community standard, is exactly the right epistemic register.

**P2 — The evaluation architecture, including the negative results, is a field-level contribution.** Pre-registered gates with git-verified commit times (S11), a shuffled-coordinate null under every headline metric, matched-call-count comparison as the only licensed head-to-head, a failed pre-registered gate reported as a failure (trusted-novel 48.5% vs 70%, S7), a withdrawn clustering claim with its ablation shown (S10), and — the sharpest result in the paper — the S12 demonstration that an atlas-trained scorer gains 16 atlas points while losing 8–11% against long reads. Fig S12's two derived rules (long reads as read-out only; anti-atlas-prior guard on future defaults) should be quoted by every future benchmark in this area. The negative results here read as results, not as padding; see M11 for the one place the meta-narrative overreaches.

**P3 — The replication discipline and the engineering that makes it usable.** Patient (never library) as the unit of replication, an opposite-direction veto, the entire pipeline re-run under patient-wise shuffles, both directions of cross-donor concordance reported with the arithmetic ceiling named (84.2% / 39.9% vs 44.4%), and the un-tuned second-library gate pass (107/108 parameters identical). The compute fix (293.7 GB → 12.53 GB peak RSS; cohort 9:06 → 1:06 h) turns the method from a big-node curiosity into something a lab can run, and wall times disclose their concurrency — a small honesty most papers skip.

---

## 2. Major concerns

**M1 — The headline superiority claim rests principally on the metric the paper itself proves gameable. (Results Fig 2; Discussion; Fig S12.)** The paper's central methodological lesson is that curated-atlas agreement rewards atlas-shaped priors, and that long-read truth is the read-out that catches this. Yet the atlas-independent Kinnex read-out is reported for exactly two tools: PeakATail (0.7647) and polyApipe (0.3895). Sierra, scAPAtrap, SCAPTURE and scUTRquant have no long-read concordance anywhere in the manuscript. By the paper's own argument, the panel ranking in Fig 2 is incomplete evidence: a reader cannot exclude that some competitor's calls, while less atlas-concordant, are more long-read-concordant — precisely the failure mode S12 documents for a model variant of PeakATail itself. **Required:** Kinnex concordance (after de-duplication, see M8) for every tool in the panel, at shipped operating points and at matched call counts, presented as a co-primary axis next to atlas agreement. This is the single revision most likely to make the paper's headline claim as strong as its discipline (see §4).

**M2 — Possible circularity in the recall denominator; the recipe is ambiguous as written. (Methods "Benchmark protocol"; Fig 2 legend.)** R_det restricts the atlas to "genes detected by the run", with detected gene IDs taken "from the run's `annotatedpas.bed`" — a PeakATail output file. Fig 2's legend says "identical denominators for every tool". These two statements together imply that the gene universe for *all* tools' recall is defined by *PeakATail's* detection. If so, the denominator is conditioned on the method under test: genes PeakATail misses entirely are removed from every tool's denominator (inflating PeakATail's R_det relative to a tool-neutral universe), and genes a competitor detects but PeakATail does not are excluded from that competitor's numerator opportunity. If instead each tool gets its own denominator, "identical denominators" is false and the R_det columns are not comparable. **Required:** state exactly whose detection defines the 285,136 / 126,686 / 268,097 denominators; rebuild them from a tool-independent source (the CellRanger/STARsolo gene-count matrix at a stated expression threshold) and show the ordering is unchanged. [ALSO A DEFECT: reproducibility gap in Methods.]

**M3 — Ten permutations is thin support for a zero-replication headline. (Fig 6; Methods "Cohort switch replication".)** The paper is admirably careful to report "none in 10 label-shuffle nulls (empirical p ≤ 0.091, the 10-permutation floor)" and never an FDR. But the abstract's most striking claim — 15,942 replicated switches, zero null replication — is supported by a null resolved only to p ≈ 0.09. With the v2 cohort run now at 1:06 h, one hundred patient-wise permutations is roughly five days of compute on the authors' own numbers: run them (or derive an analytical bound from the per-permutation single-patient call distribution, whose mean of 1.1 the funnel already reports). Separately, the relationship between the 58.9 M library-level null tests and the 2,128,711 patient-level hypotheses needs one connecting sentence — as written, a careful reader has to reverse-engineer that tests are per-library and hypotheses are patient-aggregated.

**M4 — The calibration failure is shown for one tool and one dataset; the field-level claim needs at least one external anchor. (Fig 4; Background ¶3.)** The Background asserts that shipped differential-usage statistics are rarely measured under a true null — and then measures only PeakATail's. The obvious, feasible experiment: run Sierra's DEXSeq-based test (and, if practical, one other competitor test) through the *identical* label-permutation harness on the same mouse. If competitor tests are also anti-conservative, the paper's field-level diagnosis is demonstrated rather than asserted; if they are calibrated, the reader deserves to know that too. The authors also concede the calibration verdict rests on one mouse, one tissue, with very large true effects; a second calibration dataset (e.g., PBMC cell-type labels, where effects are smaller) would show the 3.0% null rate is not tissue luck. As it stands, "only one of six configurations controls the FDR" is a solid result about `peakatail switch diff` and an unsupported insinuation about everyone else.

**M5 — The recall-deficit framing is honest but twice over-reaches; survivability depends on matched-N curves the paper shows for only one competitor. (Results "The recall budget, measured"; Fig 3.)** Honest: the numbers are stated against named denominators, the F1-optimum concession is explicit, and the 20.0-sites-per-gene-vs-4.0 framing is fair. Two over-reaches: (a) "the hard ceiling for anything downstream of peak calling" (R_det 0.34) is a ceiling of *PeakATail's candidate generator*, not of the data — polyApipe's higher recall on the same BAM proves the reads support more; one clause making that explicit is needed, or the sentence will be read as "no tool could do better". (b) The 44.6% Kinnex corroboration of the detected-gene atlas cannot bound what is real: it is donor-mismatched (the text concedes this) *and* thresholded on un-deduplicated support (M8), so its use as "the denominator is itself an upper bound on what any tool can find" is rhetorically stronger than its evidence. On survivability: yes — at matched N the tool's frontier is competitive — but the matched-N analysis is run only against polyApipe. Sierra and scAPAtrap presumably ship usable ranking columns; either truncate them too or state why only polyApipe can be truncated (Methods currently explains the exclusion only for SCAPTURE). Finally, the pbmc4k depth confound: downsample `pbmc_10k_v3` to pbmc4k depth and re-run the default — this cleanly separates depth from library/donor effects in the cross-donor asymmetry and costs half an hour by the paper's own timings.

**M6 — Spermatogenesis: three reproducibility/statistics gaps. (Fig 5; Methods "Spermatogenesis analysis".)** (a) The headline z-scores (10.5 / 6.4) come from 20 label shuffles; a z of 10.5 estimated from a 20-draw null SD assumes a normality the reader cannot check. Report the empirical floor (p ≤ 1/21) alongside, or run enough shuffles (≥200) that the tail claim is empirical. (b) PDUI is defined as distal/(proximal + distal) "at the PAS pair", but the rule selecting *which* proximal/distal pair per gene is nowhere stated — most-used pair? most extreme pair? This is unreproducible as written. [ALSO A DEFECT.] (c) The literature-panel non-reproduction (4/16 shorten in both mice) is presented under a section promising that "each [negative result] names the alternative explanation tested and excluded" — but the most obvious alternative here is not excluded: PAS-to-gene assignment in overlapping loci is under open revision (issue #99), and a 16-gene panel measured through gene assignment could fail because the measurement is mis-assigned, not because the literature is wrong. Manually verify the PAS-to-gene assignment for those 16 genes (a one-afternoon check) or soften "the panel does not reproduce" to "does not reproduce under our assignment".

**M7 — Gene-level cohort statements lean on the assignment the paper declares defective. (Fig 6e; Results Fig 6 disclosures.)** The paper rightly refuses a ranked gene list because 40% of top-30 gene rows mis-assign — and then reports 2,883 genes, 10,415 (pair, gene) combinations, and Fig 6e's 57.8%/61.5% 3′UTR-context fractions, all computed through the same assignment machinery. Either quantify these numbers' robustness to the known failure mode (e.g., recompute on the non-overlapping-locus subset) or mark them with the same caution that kills the gene list. The PAS-level replication statistics are unaffected and correctly firewalled.

**M8 — The long-read truth set is unreproducible and un-deduplicated as used. (Methods "Long-read truth construction"; Fig S5 pending.)** No accession, sample identifier, or download source is given for the Kinnex x3p and GEM-X data — "publicly available PacBio Kinnex long-read PBMC data" is not a citation a reader can follow, and there is not even a [[CITE]] slot for the datasets themselves (only for the Kinnex platform paper). [ALSO A DEFECT.] Worse, the support thresholds count alignment records with ~10.5% known (CB, UMI) duplication, the paper flags the thresholds as "slightly optimistic", and the de-duplication is exactly what S5 is pending on. The headline 0.7647 concordance, the polyApipe 0.3895, the two-truth 15.10%/49.50% composite, and S7's 48.5% all inherit this. De-duplicate the truth, recompute these four numbers, and state the deltas; do not submit with the truth set the paper itself describes as awaiting correction.

**M9 — Assorted Methods gaps a reader could not reproduce. (Methods, several subsections.)** (i) Read acceptance requires a read to "align over at most `--seq-len` nucleotides" — does this exclude spliced alignments whose genomic span exceeds read length? If so, what fraction of reads is dropped and why is that safe near 3′ ends? Define the quantity (query bases vs reference span). (ii) The GEX stage labels are "marker-panel argmax" but the marker panel is never listed (the cohort's non-immune marker gates are listed — the asymmetry is conspicuous). (iii) "Curated immune labels" for Laughney: curated by whom, from where? (iv) BH is "per cell-type pair" — per pair *per library*, presumably; say so. (v) For two-library patients, how is a within-patient direction conflict between the two libraries handled by the "counts once" rule? (vi) All of this ultimately depends on the companion repository, which is not yet public (the Zenodo placeholder says so). Genome Biology reviewers will expect working access at review time, not at proof.

**M10 — The submission is structurally incomplete: S5, S6 and Table T7 are "in preparation". (Supplementary information; Methods "Competitors".)** A Method-track submission cannot carry two supplementary figure slots and the competitor-parameters table as placeholders. T7 is not optional garnish — every competitor claim in Fig 2/3 ("their own documented workflows", "retry/skip caveats") is vouched for by a table that does not yet exist in the draft. Complete T7 and either finish S5/S6 or remove the slots and renumber. (I note S5's pending reason is the same de-duplication as M8 — resolving one resolves both.)

**M11 — The pre-registration meta-narrative occasionally tips from evidence into memoir. (Discussion ¶3; Fig S11; Results "What did not work".)** The negative results themselves are strengths (P2). But the Discussion's twelve-minutes-after-failure anecdote, the Δ = 0.000000 story told twice (Discussion and Methods), and headings like "The discipline is drawn, not asserted" spend the reader's attention on the authors' virtue rather than the method's properties. Genome Biology readers will extend trust on the strength of S11's timeline; they do not need the narration. Condense Discussion ¶3 to roughly half its length, keep the two examples as one sentence each, and let the figure carry the audit.

**M12 — Independence assumptions of the cell-level Fisher test deserve explicit treatment. (Methods "Differential APA test"; Fig 4 caveats.)** Fisher on cells treats cells within a library as independent; ambient RNA, doublets and clonal structure violate this, and the label-permutation null tests exchangeability only — a limitation the Fig 4 caveat states but the Methods and Discussion never absorb. The honest defence is already in the paper's architecture: within-library miscalibration from dependence is exactly what the cross-patient replication filter and patient-wise shuffles are designed to survive. Say that, in Methods, in one paragraph — and consider a per-patient pseudobulk sensitivity analysis on the replicated set as a supplement.

## 3. Minor issues

1. **Terminology drift on the guarded metric:** Results Fig 3 ¶2 uses "the de novo precision ordering" and "the precision lead", and Fig 3b's legend likewise — the paper elsewhere is scrupulous about "atlas-agreement precision"/"P@100". Use the guarded term at every occurrence; these two paragraphs are where a hostile reader will quote you saying bare "precision". (The matched-N one-liner's wording is fine — it is the licensed formulation.) [ALSO A DEFECT under the project's own language rules.]
2. **20,000 vs 20,320:** Key points and Results say precision leads "above 20,000 calls"; Fig 2's legend says "above 20,320". Pick one number and use it everywhere. [ALSO A DEFECT: internal inconsistency.]
3. **A new composite result first appears in Discussion:** the two-truth hard-false-positive comparison (15.10% vs 49.50%) is a result and belongs in Results (the paper's own framing document slots it there); Discussion may then interpret it.
4. **"Dominates" (Results Fig 2 ¶2):** Pareto-dominance is presumably meant; either say "is on a curve that passes above polyApipe's operating point on both axes" or define the term. As written it skirts the comparative-language restraint the paper otherwise observes.
5. **"92-fold specificity" (Results Fig 1 ¶1):** "in the measured prototype" — measured how, on what? One clause or a pointer; currently it is the only unexplained measurement in the section.
6. **"0 of 40 null draws" (Results Fig 5 ¶2):** 20 shuffles are stated; which 40 draws? (Two tails of 20? Both mice pooled?) One parenthesis fixes it.
7. **Confidence intervals are asymmetric across headline numbers:** the Kinnex concordance gets a Wilson 95% CI; the four gate P@100 values (0.7062/0.8279/0.7450/0.7572) get none. Add binomial CIs to the gate table.
8. **pbmc4k donor identity is checkable, cheaply:** genotype concordance from common SNPs in the two public BAMs would convert "presumptively a second individual" into a demonstrated fact (or a demonstrated confound). Strongly encouraged; either outcome is informative.
9. **Fig 3c competitor rows are v1-era measurements against a v2 PeakATail arm** — disclosed in the legend, but the mismatch should be visible in the panel itself (greyed rows or an asterisk), not only in the caption.
10. **Abstract:** "at matched call count PeakATail leads on recall at every budget tested" — name polyApipe as the comparator in the abstract; unanchored, it reads as a field-wide claim.
11. **Cell-type pair bookkeeping (Fig 6):** state explicitly that the 59 pairs are within-library pairs pooled at patient level, and how many libraries contribute to a typical pair; panel (b)'s composition caveat hints at this but Methods never defines the pair universe.
12. **Title:** the question is good; the subtitle is a compound of three claims. "Precision-first calling and pre-registered evaluation of single-cell poly(A) sites with PeakATail" loses nothing.
13. **Key points partially duplicate the abstract** (the matched-N sentence appears nearly verbatim in both); GB allows this but trimming the overlap buys words for M1's new panel.
14. **`--polya-min-umis` remains 1 at the caller while ≥2 molecules is the pre-registered output** — disclosed twice, correctly; consider making the default output of the released tool match the paper's default, or state why not, since users will run the tool, not the paper.

---

## 4. Decision

**Major revision** — and I want to be explicit that this is an enthusiastic major revision. The evaluation discipline is the contribution the field needs, the negative results are genuine results, and nothing in my concerns suggests the central claims are wrong; they suggest the claims are not yet supported to the standard the paper itself sets. M1/M8 (panel-wide, de-duplicated long-read axis), M2 (denominator provenance), M3 (more permutations), M6b (PDUI pair rule) and M10 (pending supplements) are required; M4 and M5's downsampling control are strongly requested; the rest are at the editors' discretion.

**The single change most likely to improve the paper:** extend the de-duplicated long-read (Kinnex) concordance read-out to every tool in the benchmark panel, at shipped and matched call counts, and present it as a co-primary accuracy axis beside atlas agreement. The paper's most original result (S12) argues that atlas agreement alone cannot rank callers; the moment the authors apply that lesson to their own headline comparison, the claim "highest precision of any de novo tool" stops resting on the one metric the paper has shown can be gamed — and, on the evidence of the 0.7647-vs-0.3895 fragment already in hand, the claim will very likely get stronger, not weaker.
